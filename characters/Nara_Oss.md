# Nara Oss

Nara Oss is a novice of the Obsidian Synod working in Black Horizon release
rites when the events of *The Mercy Doctrine* take place. She is trained to
understand forgetting as compassion: a disciplined release of weight that no
longer has work except suffering.

Her crisis begins when an abandoned station proves not to be wholly abandoned.
One living inhabitant refuses release and asks that the station's pain remain as
warning. Nara's choice is not a simple conversion away from Synod belief. She
still believes needless suffering may be released. She also learns that some
pain points toward preventable harm, and that calling such pain a burden can
become another form of violence.

## Cognitive Profile

### Strengths

- Careful record-keeper; notices categories and procedural language under stress
- Sincere doctrinal discipline, which makes her dissent costly rather than
  impulsive
- Able to distinguish chosen release from institutional convenience when forced
  to witness both in one room

### Vulnerabilities

- Young enough in Synod practice to trust elders before she trusts her own
  judgment
- Carries testimony that may become socially and metaphysically heavy over time
- Risks becoming useful to anti-Synod institutions in ways that could distort
  the witness she preserved

## Narrative Function

Nara gives the Obsidian Synod interiority without making its doctrine harmless.
Her story shows release as sometimes merciful and sometimes coercive, depending
on consent, purpose, and what the memory still does in the world. She preserves
a warning not because memory is always good, but because this particular memory
still points.

## Post-Story Status

Nara leaves the abandoned station carrying a ledger entry that denies total
oblivion for the station's pressure-door warning. Her status inside the Synod is
unresolved. The retained testimony remains a burden she has accepted, not a
victory over doctrine.
