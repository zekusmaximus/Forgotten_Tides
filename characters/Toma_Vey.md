# Toma Vey

Toma Vey is a freight auditor in Heavonly's lower-deck intake systems. His work is
ordinary by Heavonly standards: count physical cargo, reconcile manifests, delay
what does not match, and absorb blame when upper-level categories fail to describe
what has actually arrived.

In *The Inventory of Absence*, Toma receives a sealed lot from a quarantined
mid-world route. The containers are physically present but administratively marked
as no cargo. When the lot begins lowering local coherence and erasing its own count,
Toma files it under his personal identity record so Heavonly's systems must keep a
number attached to it.

## Post-Story Status

Toma's filing preserves the lot long enough for Archivist witness and restricted
custody, but it damages the reliability of his own institutional record. Residence
access, meal allocation, shift history, and personal identifiers become less stable
because the absence he preserved now has a handle in his name.

## Narrative Function

Toma represents the lower-deck infrastructure perspective developed for the flagship
novel: ordinary workers who see the movement of atrocity before institutions can or
will name it. He does not expose the conspiracy. He keeps one trace from becoming
zero.
