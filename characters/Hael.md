# Hael
Senior Reliquarist, Archivist Orders
Station Assignment: Tessarine Vault, Helios Drift outer sleeve

Brother Hael spent thirty years in the Tessarine audit hall and ran its floor without
raising his voice. He is the source of two teachings Inya Solven carries: that the cradle
does not tell a Reliquarist what a memory is, only whether the memory believes itself; and
that the only compliment worth giving a junior is *hands*. In *The Reliquarist's Doubt* he
notices what her junior seal cost her and chooses not to ask.

## Anchor Structure

Seven knots at the opening of story-0022. None at its close.

Named as given, in order: the first cradle he tuned, with the cracked plate; the Vault's
night bell before it was changed, three strokes and the third late; his father's cough from
the next room when he was nine; a name he would not say; the Vault-Master's voice reading
the lintel over him at his ordination; the first clean pitch he heard under a ring, kept
forty years as the reason for everything after. The seventh he gave without naming it, and
afterward did not know it either.

## State After Story-0022

Catastrophic anchor loss. His body stands where he left it, breathing, burned at the
thumbs. His face is a face. The room will not hold him: eyes slide off him; the name
*Hael* spoken toward him falls and does not land. The only purchase the silo bank has on
him is the name Ilka Marsk scored into the brass of the Second Sleeve housing, which Inya
Solven deepens with her stylus every few breaths. This is the state mechanics/ANCHOR_THEORY.md
calls a conceptual ghost. It is irreversible.

## Relationships

- **Inya Solven** (char-0010): his junior and the person who keeps saying his name. One of
  her three anchor knots is the shape of his voice calling her *hands*. It still answers.
- **Ilka Marsk** (char-0028): the Navigant who had the sentence that would have stopped him
  and said *name them as they go* instead.

## Narrative Function

Hael is the first on-page demonstration of what the Orders' doctrine costs at its limit.
Pledged stones could thicken the Second Sleeve; only a living, attending person could bind
it. He spends himself completely, consensually, and exactly, and the universe does what the
mechanics say it does: it stops being able to hold him. He is not a martyr in the story's
telling. He is a man who said *stones don't attend, I do* and was right.
