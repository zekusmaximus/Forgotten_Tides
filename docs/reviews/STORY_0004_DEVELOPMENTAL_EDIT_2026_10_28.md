## Assessment

The grandfather's correction, Tess's careful assay, and Yenu's final recitation
gave the draft an emotional center. The main weakness was the shape of the
choice. Yenu refused the Consortium and announced that she would hear and
release the stone at Halix. Roughly a third of the story then confirmed a
decision already made. The failing frame was described in prices and softening
corners, while the adults closest to Yenu approved or quietly accepted her
choice. The ending therefore carried less cost than the premise promised.

## Revision made

| Developmental problem | Revision in the draft |
| --- | --- |
| The choice ended at Halix. | Yenu refuses Aurel's permanent-custody contract, but keeps his timed offer and asks Tess to seek private buyers. She returns with a sale still possible. Her direct hearing beside her grandfather is the irreversible choice. |
| The repair cost stayed abstract. | Hol-Vren measures the bowing sleeping-cell corner, moves two children to the galley, and braces it. The filament and retuning cost eight cycles of pooled wages; Tess's twelve-to-fourteen-cycle valuation is a range, not cash already offered. |
| Every adult validated Yenu. | Her grandfather admits he considered selling the stone and says her mother may be right to want the filament. Her mother names the lost price, the displaced children, and the work still due. Neither decides for Yenu. |
| The assay and intended hearing blurred together. | Tess exposes a trace through her ring while Yenu keeps her hands away. At the bedside, Yenu holds the stone and answers the voice. Only that chosen hearing fills and closes this stone's hollow. No general rule for other bequests is asserted. |
| The grandmother remained a generic warm voice. | Her full recording gives an ordinary, specific correction about the uneven tuning chair. Yenu can pass that detail to her mother. |
| The death confused chosen thinning with natural dying. | The grandfather dies naturally, in his own bed, with his care preference clear and the medic confirming his death. The separate Drift Clade practice of stopping stays distinct. |
| The prose explained its themes and resolved the cost twice. | The revision cuts repeated cultural interpretation and aphoristic dialogue, leaves one slight response from the frame, and ends with the sleeping-cell brace and repair bill still present. |

The story's fixed outcomes remain: Yenu is sixteen and untrained as a tuner;
she refuses Aurel, hears her grandmother with her grandfather, releases the
closed carrier, and begins his accurate recitation after his death. His spoken
correction remains “could not afford to,” which draft `story-0021` uses later.
The Halix Synod booth remains an unverified rumor, and Aurel reveals no
concealed Consortium node failure.
